NewsTue Jan 1, 2002 3:15 pm GMT · EuropeFirst day: busy machines, few euro paymentsrecalled by 1 member

Germany's Dresdner Bank says only 20-30 of its 1,100 machines failed to give euros in the first half hour; Deutsche Bank says 98.5% of its 1,700 machines were ready and 10,000 withdrawals were made in 30 minutes. An ECB spokesman expects 80-85% of machines in the euro area to be converted by tonight. In Brussels and on the Champs-Elysees many machines had no euros at first. A Paris bakery says only three customers paid in euros by 9am. Even in Zurich and Basel, outside the EU, hundreds queued at midnight to buy euros. Source

NewsTue Jan 1, 2002 4:00 pm GMT · Dublin, IrelandDublin queues for euros - with free whiskeyrecalled by 3 members

The Central Bank in Dame Street, the only bank open in Ireland today, is changing punts into euros free of charge, with up to 250 people queuing at a time and champagne, whiskey, tea and coffee on offer. Taoiseach Bertie Ahern says more than five million customer accounts have been converted, and inspectors will check how shops convert their prices. Source

It is quite standard for customer footfall to start slowly on a public holiday, particularly when people have existing national cash to spend before dipping into their new notes. A bakery in Paris seeing only three euro payments before nine o'clock simply reflects that people are using up what is already in their purses, not that the system has jammed.

The operational reality remains that the conversion mechanisms have behaved as intended. When Deutsche Bank and Dresdner report failure rates of under two per cent across thousands of terminals, and account migrations pass without a hitch, the infrastructure has done its part. Dual circulation exists precisely to buffer the retail trade while the public gradually switches over. Cashiers and shoppers will naturally adjust their pace over the coming days, just as they always do during administrative transitions.

NewsWed Jan 2, 2002 1:08 am GMT · Brussels, Belgium / Madrid, SpainMadrid and Brussels: queues at the few banks openrecalled by 1 member

In Brussels, cash machines handled about 600 withdrawals a minute in the two hours after midnight, and the Commission's press bar ran out of euro coins. In Madrid, only about 60 bank branches opened on New Year's Day; queues stretched along the pavements and some people waited two hours, while a long line formed outside the Bank of Spain. Source

NewsWed Jan 2, 2002 1:08 am GMT · Dublin, Ireland / Newry, Northern IrelandAhern pays with Belgian coins; Newry takes three currenciesnot recalled by anyone

Bertie Ahern bought milk and three pears at his local Dublin newsagent with Belgian euro coins, to show that coins from any euro country are valid in Ireland. The first Irish machine to give euros was a Bank of Ireland machine at Dublin airport, at about 6am. Across the border in Newry, Northern Ireland, shops that already took sterling and punts now also take euros. Source

NewsWed Jan 2, 2002 1:08 am GMT · Rome, Italy / Athens, GreeceItaly and Greece slow to startrecalled by 2 members

By midday on New Year's Day only half a million Italians had taken euros from cash machines, and two-thirds of machines were still giving lire. Many Italian businesses had not stocked up with euro coins. In Greece, many street kiosks refused even to give change in euros, and most Greeks plan to keep spending drachmas until 28 February. Source
