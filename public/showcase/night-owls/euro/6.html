■ Posted: Mon Dec 31, 2001 11:49 pm    Post subject: Re: Euro notes and coins arrive on 1 January - who's ready?quote
One must remember that commercial trade relies upon bilateral agreement rather than compulsory service. A customer who presents an excessive denomination cannot compel a merchant to deplete an entire cash float, strike or no strike. In such routine circumstances, the buyer simply provides an alternative payment method, retrieves smaller coins from home, or departs without the item. It is a completely standard retail contingency. Furthermore, with hundreds of millions of euro starter kits already in private circulation across the continent, households possess substantial quantities of small coinage ready for daily transactions. The commercial mechanisms are thoroughly prepared to absorb ordinary customer friction.

■ Posted: Mon Dec 31, 2001 11:50 pm    Post subject: Re: Euro notes and coins arrive on 1 January - who's ready?quote
That is the long and short of it. Retail trade is built upon mutual cooperation, not an unyielding contest of wills. If a customer happens to lack the right amount, they simply step aside, check their pockets, or come back later.

We ought to remember as well that cash drawers are not sealed off from the world. As the morning progresses, other shoppers will be paying with smaller coins and notes, continuously replenishing the till. A shopkeeper does not depend solely upon morning visits to a bank branch to keep trade flowing; money circulates naturally through the till with every transaction.

The authorities have also noted that billions of these new notes and tens of billions of coins were shipped out well beforehand. When you combine that vast volume of currency with the simple good sense of ordinary shopkeepers, the dire predictions of paralysis quickly evaporate. A bit of patience on both sides of the counter is all that is ever required.
