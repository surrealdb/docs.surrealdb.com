Besides the twelve EU countries, the euro will be used in Monaco, San Marino, the Vatican, Andorra, Kosovo, Montenegro and French territories such as Reunion, Martinique, Guadeloupe and French Guiana. Reunion, in the Indian Ocean, will see it first. Monaco, San Marino and the Vatican may mint their own euro coins, and Vatican coins with the Pope's face are expected to go to collectors. Europol fears counterfeiters will use Kosovo and Montenegro to spread fake notes. Source

When the fake money circulates and bank strikes in places like France leave people high and dry at the counter, the authorities will turn right around and tell the public that physical cash is too unsafe to keep using.
Law enforcement agencies routinely identify high-risk jurisdictions during any major currency transition, and monitoring peripheral regions is standard practice rather than evidence of an inevitable breakdown. The vast majority of physical cash remains strictly within domestic banking networks, where clearing houses and sorting centres detect irregularities before notes enter widespread retail circulation.

Industrial action over wage negotiations in France is equally unremarkable at the end of a fiscal year. Bank staff unions naturally use significant public deadlines to strengthen their bargaining position. These are standard trade union tactics and basic cross-border policing matters, not an orchestrated campaign to abolish tangible currency. Our local arrangements here in Rotterdam remain entirely orderly.
