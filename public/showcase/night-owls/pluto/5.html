TheyKnow wrote:
Ask yourself why NASA fired a seven-hundred-million-dollar rocket straight toward Pluto in January if the establishment really considered it just a stray pebble in the backwoods.
Kevin is entirely correct. Missions are funded to investigate geological and physical phenomena, not to validate an entry in an encyclopedia. Exploration does not depend on a bureaucratic honour. We launched spacecraft to explore comets and asteroids long before anyone considered defining the word planet.

Pluto is situated right in the heart of the Kuiper belt. In dynamic terms, it does not dominate its orbital neighbourhood in the slightest; it shares that zone with an entire population of icy bodies and remains locked in a 3:2 mean-motion resonance with Neptune. Claiming that an orbital dominance criterion was engineered merely to target an American probe ignores decades of observational work since 1992.

Punchcard is sensible to remind us that the Executive Committee must still deliberate on how to merge these texts before Thursday. But distinguishing between bodies that gravitationally control their orbital territory and those that simply drift within a vast swarm is ordinary, sound mechanics. Applying proper physics to taxonomy is not an ideological manoeuvre.
