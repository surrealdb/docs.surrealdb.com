NewsThu Aug 24, 2006 1:14 pm GMT · Prague, Czech RepublicNo new names at this stage, says Bell Burnellnot recalled by anyone

At a final question session, Jocelyn Bell Burnell rejected 'planetino', saying no new names would be introduced now. Small fixes are planned, including quotation marks around 'dwarf planet' and a separate place for satellites. She told Nature it is very hard to predict how the vote will go. The closing session is starting, with other resolutions to vote on first. Source

NewsThu Aug 24, 2006 1:35 pm GMT · Prague, Czech RepublicPluto demoted: 'Pluto is dead'recalled by 2 members

IAU members in Prague have voted for a definition under which Pluto is no longer a planet. Mike Brown, following the vote by teleconference, says there are finally, officially, eight planets. Only 424 astronomers were there on the last day to vote, out of about 10,000 professional astronomers. Alan Stern, head of the New Horizons mission, says he is embarrassed for astronomy, that the 'cleared the neighbourhood' rule fails for Earth and Jupiter, and that a petition against the decision is already circulating. Owen Gingerich, in Boston and unable to vote, calls the result confusing and unfortunate and hopes for electronic voting in future. Source

NewsThu Aug 24, 2006 1:43 pm GMT · Prague, Czech Republic5A carried, 5B rejected: eight planets onlyrecalled by 4 members

Resolution 5A, defining planets, dwarf planets and small solar system bodies, was carried by a clear majority of raised yellow cards. To show the categories, Jocelyn Bell Burnell produced a blue balloon for the planets and a cereal box and a stuffed Disney Pluto for the dwarf planets. Resolution 5B, which would have made 'planet' an umbrella term covering dwarf planets, got 91 votes in favour and far more against. So there are eight planets. Source

Resolution 6A, making Pluto the prototype of a new category of objects beyond Neptune, passed by 237 votes to 157, with 17 abstentions. NewsThu Aug 24, 2006 4:00 pm GMT · Prague, Czech RepublicIAU: eight planets; Ceres, Pluto and 2003 UB313 are dwarf planetsrecalled by 4 members

The IAU confirms that the solar system now has eight planets, Mercury to Neptune, and that 'planets' and 'dwarf planets' are separate classes. The first dwarf planets are Ceres, Pluto and 2003 UB313, with about a dozen candidates on a watchlist. Resolution 5A passed by a large majority without a count; 5B had 91 in favour and many more against; 6A passed 237-157 with 17 abstentions; 6B failed 183-186. More than 2,500 astronomers attended the General Assembly. Source
