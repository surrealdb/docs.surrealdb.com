It is already running into severe mathematical and physical objections. Gregory Laughlin pointed out just today that relying on the external barycentre rule produces absurdities: as the Moon slowly drifts outwards, the Earth-Moon barycentre will eventually migrate into open space, turning our Moon into an independent planet under this draft. In eccentric orbits, a body could even alternate between being a moon and a planet.

That is precisely what happens when a committee ignores orbital dynamics in favour of static geometry. A definition that requires special carve-outs and produces orbital boundary anomalies cannot last. If the floor takes the physics seriously, Pluto belongs with the rest of the Kuiper belt population.

TheyKnow wrote:
Seven top scholars spend months behind closed doors in Paris, and we are supposed to believe not one of them noticed what happens to the Moon or an eccentric orbit?
The presence of edge cases in an initial draft is a standard hazard of mathematical modeling, not proof of clandestine plotting. Committees frequently focus on resolving a specific set of parameters - in this case, reconciling Pluto's status with newly discovered trans-Neptunian bodies - while failing to calculate the broader boundary conditions.

The examples highlighted by Laughlin demonstrate why an astronomical definition cannot depend on static spatial geometry alone. A criterion where an object's taxonomy might oscillate between satellite and planet depending on orbital eccentricity is mathematically untenable. It produces an absurd taxonomy rather than an analytical framework.

When dynamical astronomers argue that a planet must gravitationally govern its zone, they are applying established celestial mechanics. Pluto sharing its resonance with an entire population of Kuiper belt objects is a physical fact. If the assembly amends the resolution to incorporate orbital dynamics instead of arbitrary barycentre thresholds, it will be the result of rational critique overcoming a poorly formulated text, exactly as scientific peer review is designed to work.
