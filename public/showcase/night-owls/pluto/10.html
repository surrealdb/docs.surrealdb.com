Astronomers have merely sorted objects by how the solar system actually operates. A body that commands its own orbit belongs in one ledger, whilst bodies that travel within a belt belong in another. Ceres went through the very same transition in the nineteenth century without the sky falling down.

A petition or a black sash in a display case will not overturn the basic mathematics. The dynamicists have given us an orderly rule, and the textbooks will be much easier to understand for it.

■ Posted: Fri Aug 25, 2006 9:04 pm    Post subject: Re: Astronomers vote on what a planet is - is Pluto safe?quote
It is worth separating the procedural noise from the physics.

1. Attendance at the closing session of an academic conference is always lower than the opening plenary; that is standard across every discipline, not an orchestrated coup.

2. The distinction between an isolated, gravitationally dominant body and a swarm object is not bookkeeping, but orbital mechanics. If a definition ignored whether an object shares a belt, we would be obliged to admit Ceres, 2003 UB313, and dozens of comparable trans-Neptunian bodies currently on the watchlist into the primary register.

Petitions and planetarium sashes are theatrical reactions to an inevitable physical sorting. Pluto remains an exceptionally interesting Kuiper belt object, but the dynamical baseline is sound.
