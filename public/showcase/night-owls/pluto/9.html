■ Posted: Thu Aug 24, 2006 10:28 pm    Post subject: Re: Astronomers vote on what a planet is - is Pluto safe?quote
Raising objections about Jupiter or Earth merely misinterprets the dynamical physics involved. Gravitational dominance does not require a planet to vacuum up every single particle along its path. Jupiter contains Trojan asteroids precisely because its immense gravity controls and traps them at its Lagrange points. It controls its zone entirely, whereas Pluto's orbit is directly dictated by Neptune's orbital resonance.

Alan Stern is naturally protective of his mission, but scientific definitions are not written to flatter the targets of specific spacecraft. New Horizons was funded to study a fascinating, icy frontier, and finding that Pluto represents a whole population of similar trans-Neptunian objects makes the science richer, not diminished.

As for the procedure, a lively floor debate with visual aids is entirely typical for a large international conference. It does not invalidate a decisive vote. Delegates considered the proposals, rejected an awkward compromise that would have expanded the planetary register indefinitely, and chose an objective physical standard. The official taxonomy has been updated, textbook publishers have their marching orders, and the discipline will proceed on firmer ground.

Nobody is saying Pluto is not a real world though! It has heaps of amazing features, atmosphere and moons, but so does Titan and nobody calls Titan a planet either. Being a dwarf planet doesn't make it a fake object or an insult to geologists, it just describes where it sits in the architecture of the solar system.

If we only looked at geology and roundness, Ceres would be a planet, Charon would be a planet, and 2003 UB313 would be a planet too. We would end up with dozens or hundreds of them, and then kids at school wouldn't be able to make any sense of how the solar system actually works dynamically. The eight true planets are completely unique because they dominate their space, while Pluto is part of a massive swarm of icy objects out past Neptune.

The vote in Prague gave us an honest, workable taxonomy that fits both orbital reality and classification. Trying to pretend the vote didn't happen won't change the fact that Pluto lives in a crowded belt, not in isolation like Neptune or Jupiter. It makes the solar system way more interesting to teach, not less.
