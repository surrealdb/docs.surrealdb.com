It is genuinely good to see you checked in safe and sound, kookaburra99, and I am glad your evening went off without a hitch... Nobody wants to see people sitting in the dark, truly. But consumer dial-up getting a handshake through a city exchange just shows the front door is open, my friends. That is a very different animal from the deep industrial controls, pipeline monitoring, and rural feeder lines that quietly do the heavy lifting in the background.

Looking at network ping times from Dublin or watching city streetlamps stay on doesn't tell us how the main water treatment valves or substation relays hold up over the next twenty-four hours. A lot of those systems do not trip instantly... They buffer, they hold logs in memory, and then they fault when the first scheduled maintenance polling cycle tries to write an invalid timestamp. Remember what happened in Boston just today, where an outside vendor missed major patches on entire hospital systems until a last-minute audit caught it. If that happens in a premier medical facility, think of the small rural utilities running legacy equipment without any tech staff on hand. I am still keeping a very close watch on the amateur bands here as night rolls across Asia.
