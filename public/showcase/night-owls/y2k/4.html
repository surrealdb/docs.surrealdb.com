When telemetry drops, safety interlocks fail safe and isolate circuits automatically, my friend... no human can react that fast.
A fail-safe interlock triggers only when an immediate physical signal is lost or breaches a safety threshold, such as sudden thermal expansion or line voltage collapse. It does not monitor software clock cycles.

SCADA telemetry passes raw sensor voltages across dedicated serial channels. Even if a supervisory computer prints an incorrect timestamp at the central console, the local monitoring loop still sees the continuous electrical signal. An interlock will not trip simply because a logging application is confused. As Kev and OffByOne have pointed out, operations will continue normally.

■ Posted: Thu Dec 30, 1999 9:43 am    Post subject: Re: Y2K - is it going to be bad??quote
Watchdog circuits in industrial remote terminal units are remarkably simple mechanisms by design. A watchdog timer is essentially an autonomous hardware counter that expects a periodic reset signal from the operating firmware. If that square-wave pulse arrives on schedule, the timer resets and the controller proceeds. The firmware pulse simply indicates that the processor is stepping through its execution loop, not that it possesses an accurate year or month.

Furthermore, RTU polling protocols communicate raw register values - binary status flags, analog-to-digital converter readings, and basic telemetry words. The polling cycle does not evaluate Julian dates or Gregorian calendars to decide whether a transducer voltage is valid. It verifies a parity bit or a checksum across the frame. If a central supervisory terminal hiccups while drawing a graphical display, the remote unit on the line continues servicing its local inputs and output relays without interruption.

Industrial automation engineers specifically build these devices so that telemetry dropouts do not instantly trip protective breakers. If transient comms jitter provoked an immediate emergency shutdown, the national grid would have collapsed decades ago. The physical plant will tick over as expected.

■ Posted: Thu Dec 30, 1999 12:35 pm    Post subject: Re: Y2K - is it going to be bad??quote
Listening for news from the earlier time zones tomorrow afternoon is certainly sensible, as that is precisely where any genuine operational data will first appear. If there were systemic vulnerabilities in standard industrial controllers, we would observe them promptly in New Zealand and Australia long before midnight approaches Britain or North America.

However, expecting automated systems to trip purely out of routine software anomalies misses how decoupled these layers are. When an automated control loop encounters an unexpected input or an unhandled calculation, the behaviour of the physical plant is determined by robust analogue baselines and local ladder logic, not by an arbitrary catastrophic plunge. Control engineers have always had to design for noisy industrial environments, power spikes, and intermittent line noise. A software process pausing or generating an error dialogue simply does not cause an entire distribution feeder to shed load.

There will doubtless be trivial administrative slip-ups, similar to the retail card readers reported yesterday, but conflating those with wholesale infrastructure shutdown remains entirely unfounded. We shall have empirical evidence from across the Pacific in just over twenty-four hours.
