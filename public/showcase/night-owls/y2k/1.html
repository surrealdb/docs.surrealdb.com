NewsWed Feb 4, 1998 5:00 pm GMT · Washington, United StatesClinton creates council to lead the federal Y2K effortnot recalled by anyone

President Clinton has signed Executive Order 13073, setting up the President's Council on Year 2000 Conversion. Agencies are told to make sure no critical federal programme is disrupted by the date change, and to work with state governments, industry and foreign governments. John Koskinen chairs the council and acts as the government's main spokesman on the problem. Source

NewsWed Dec 30, 1998 12:00 pm GMT · United StatesDoes the new millennium start in 2000 or 2001?not recalled by anyone

The US Naval Observatory, the country's official timekeeper, says the third millennium technically begins on 1 January 2001, because the calendar started at year 1 and has no year zero. Most people still plan to celebrate when the counter turns to 2000, and historians say it would be almost impossible to persuade the public otherwise. Source

NewsMon Mar 1, 1999 12:00 pm GMT · United StatesFed will have about $50 billion in extra cash ready for the rolloverrecalled by 4 members

The Federal Reserve says roughly $50 billion in currency above normal inventory levels will be available in case people withdraw more cash than usual before 2000. Officials stress this is a precaution, not advice to withdraw money, and warn that keeping large amounts of cash at home exposes people to theft and loss. Source

NewsFri Sep 10, 1999 12:00 pm GMT · Hawaii, United StatesThe 9/9/99 scare passes without problemsrecalled by 5 members

Computer systems worked normally on 9 September 1999, a date some feared would trip programs that use 9999 as a special 'end of data' marker. Checks of government, military, banks, utilities and hospitals in Hawaii found nothing, and officials called the scare a red herring. Experts say the 1 January 2000 change is a different and more serious test. Source

NewsTue Sep 14, 1999 6:00 pm GMT · Colorado Springs, United StatesUS and Russia agree on joint missile-warning centre for Y2Krecalled by 2 members

Defence secretaries William Cohen and Igor Sergeyev have agreed to set up the Center for Year 2000 Strategic Stability at Peterson Air Force Base in Colorado Springs. US and Russian officers will sit side by side watching missile and space launches over the date change, so that a computer fault cannot be mistaken for an attack. The centre is expected to cost about $8 million and run until mid-January 2000. Source

NewsSun Sep 19, 1999 12:00 pm GMT · Washington, United StatesState Department names countries with the biggest Y2K risksrecalled by 3 members

The State Department's Y2K representative told Congress that Eastern Europe is at particular risk because its power grids are fragile in winter. Its country reports expect possible disruption to power, heating, telephones and transport in Russia, and name health care and telecommunications as the weak points in Italy. Critics in the Senate say the reports are too vague to be useful. Source

NewsMon Oct 25, 1999 12:00 pm GMT · United StatesUS nuclear regulator says Y2K will not affect reactor safetyrecalled by 4 members

The Nuclear Regulatory Commission says the date change will not affect the safe operation or shutdown of the 103 US nuclear power plants. Safety systems were declared ready earlier in the year, and only a few plants still have work left on non-safety systems. Anti-nuclear groups warn that reactors and spent-fuel pools depend on outside electricity if the grid fails. Source

NewsTue Nov 16, 1999 5:00 pm GMT · Washington, United StatesCommerce Department puts US Y2K bill at about $100 billionrecalled by 2 members

A Commerce Department report says US businesses and government will spend about $100 billion on the Year 2000 problem between 1995 and 2001, around $365 per person, with spending peaking in 1998. It expects some glitches at small and medium firms but no lasting damage to economic growth. Commerce Secretary William Daley says the biggest cost is now behind the country. Source

NewsWed Dec 1, 1999 12:00 pm GMT · WorldwideExperts expect the lights to stay on as global repair bill reaches hundreds of billionsrecalled by 4 members

Analysts now estimate worldwide spending on the millennium bug at between $250 billion and $600 billion, and many predict that power and telephones will keep working at midnight. China, Vietnam and Latin America are named as more vulnerable. Some doom-sayers, such as writer Gary North, still advise people to store supplies and items to barter. Source

NewsSun Dec 12, 1999 12:00 pm GMT · United StatesFAA chief will be flying when midnight arrivesnot recalled by anyone

FAA administrator Jane Garvey plans to spend New Year's Eve on commercial flights, leaving Washington at 5:10 p.m. EST, changing in Dallas-Fort Worth and landing in San Francisco at 12:07 a.m. PST. She will be in the air at midnight GMT and at midnight in all four time zones of the continental United States. The FAA says she will fly coach. Source

NewsMon Dec 13, 1999 5:00 pm GMT · EuropeWatchdog lifts travel warning for Italy and Germany but keeps Russia on red listrecalled by 3 members

UK group Taskforce 2000 has moved Italy, Germany, Switzerland and the Czech Republic from 'red' to 'green' in its Y2K air travel ratings. Russia, Poland, Portugal, Finland and Hungary stay on the red list, and Luxembourg and Pakistan have been added. The group says the data is self-reported by governments and probably optimistic. Source

NewsTue Dec 14, 1999 11:00 pm GMT · Port Angeles, Washington, United StatesMan arrested at Washington border with suspected bomb materialsnot recalled by anyone

Ahmed Ressam, an Algerian living in Montreal, has been arrested at Port Angeles, Washington, while trying to enter the United States from Canada with what are believed to be bomb components. He reportedly had a motel reservation near Seattle Center, where a large New Year's Eve party is planned. Source

NewsFri Dec 17, 1999 5:00 pm GMT · Washington, United StatesKoskinen: no major national failures expected, but some problems may surface slowlyrecalled by 7 members

In a speech at the National Press Club, John Koskinen said power grids, telephone and financial networks and air traffic control are well prepared and he does not expect major national failures. He warned that some faults could take days, weeks or months to appear. A federal crisis centre near the White House will be staffed around the clock until mid-January. Source

TheyKnow wrote:
ask yourself WHY the Fed printed fifty billion in extra cash! Take it OUT.
The Federal Reserve prepared that extra currency specifically to prevent shortages if people made sudden, irrational withdrawals, not because they expect the banking infrastructure to collapse. The authorities explicitly warned that hoarding physical paper money at home simply exposes citizens to ordinary theft.

Enormous amounts of remediation work have taken place over the past several years, with hundreds of billions spent globally to test and patch critical systems. Minor software errors will inevitably surface in smaller companies, but essential utilities such as electricity and water will remain functional. A modest reserve of supplies is sensible, but hoarding cash is unnecessary.
