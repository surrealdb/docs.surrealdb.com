■ Posted: Tue Dec 28, 1999 10:28 am    Post subject: Re: Y2K - is it going to be bad??quote
Indeed. Confusing the retail interface with the industrial supply chain is where many of these panic scenarios originate. Electronic settlement networks and point-of-sale terminals are convenient modern conveniences, but they are entirely secondary to the physical logistics. Commercial distribution runs on long-established institutional credit, not instant transaction authorization at a till.

If point-of-sale verification falters at a petrol forecourt or grocery shop, it simply means slower queues and manual receipt imprinters. To jump from a temporary retail card glitch to empty supermarket shelves ignores the layered reality of wholesale distribution. The primary pipelines and depots will operate just fine.

■ Posted: Wed Dec 29, 1999 10:44 am    Post subject: Re: Y2K - is it going to be bad??quote
OffByOne has hit upon the crucial point. Protection circuits and governor controls operate on immediate physical variables, such as voltage thresholds, wave cycles, and rotational momentum. They are fundamentally closed-loop analogue or micro-controller systems designed to react to instantaneous telemetry, not chronological calendars. A turbine governor does not query the year to determine whether sixty hertz is being maintained.

The logic chips handling those real-time safety functions have no practical use for date arithmetic. Conflating timestamped logging interfaces with the direct mechanical and electrical protection loops is an easy error to make, but the underlying engineering simply does not work like that. The physical grid will remain perfectly stable.
