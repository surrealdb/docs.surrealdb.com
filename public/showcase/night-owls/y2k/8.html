Those old 1970s tapes are a fun memory, but modern automated plants do not run on manual levers and mechanical floats anymore, my friend... Pumps and pressure valves are driven by PLCs and automated loop controllers. Those devices poll sensors on set time intervals, and if an embedded chip calculates a negative time delta between cycles, the logic trips a safety interlock. It shuts the valve down to prevent a surge, or it locks a pump wide open. You don't get a bang right at the stroke of twelve - you get a supervisory controller trying to balance flow rates over an elapsed hour, hanging when the calculation fails, and quietly walking away from the throttle.

Even in Japan, that radiation monitoring computer failed at a nuclear plant after midnight, but they just quietly called it a minor glitch. The machinery doesn't have to explode to cause severe disruptions... It just takes a batch job timing out or a sensor reporting bad date values to start a cascade once local storage fills up. I am still sitting right here by the rigs listening to the bands, and we have a long way to go yet.
