Putting officers in a room together in Colorado is sensible statecraft, not an admission that the world is ending. When nations possess thousands of warheads, sitting side by side to ensure a misread satellite indicator is checked by human eyes before anyone jumps to conclusions is precisely what rational adults ought to do. It costs very little and removes uncertainty.

The same principle applies to having desks staffed in Washington or here in Toronto. The code does what it is told, to be sure, and millions of lines were painstakingly checked and corrected over the last three years to tell it the right thing. Having people on hand with a warm pot of coffee is simply how responsible enterprises oversee any major transition. It means someone is there to file the paperwork, keep an eye on the boards, and answer the phone if a local branch manager gets flustered.

We will not have to wait on speculation or rumours much longer. In a matter of hours, Auckland and Sydney will cross midnight. The lights will remain on, the amateur sets will pick up ordinary holiday chatter, and we can all carry on with our work.
