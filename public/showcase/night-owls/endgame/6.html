by quasistatic » Wed Apr 24, 2019 9:36 pm

It is quite telling when the media reports focus on apps like RunPee trying to cope with the running time. When patrons need software to plan biological breaks during a screening, the barrier to casual viewing becomes obvious.

The logistical problem for exhibitors going forward is that you cannot simply shave down the turnaround time. A film that spans just over three hours requires roughly three and a half hours of theatre occupancy per screening once advertisements, credits, and basic cleaning are accounted for. Operating around the clock on a weekend is a workable novelty for chains like AMC, but that model collapses the moment the working week begins on Monday.

Avatar could sustain modest theatre occupancies over months because it slotted easily into standard evening slots and drew people who were completely unbothered by continuity. Endgame is consuming its primary market in an unprecedented burst. Once that pool of invested viewers has been serviced, filling those lengthy slots with casual patrons will prove an entirely different challenge.

The arithmetic of total admissions remains the decisive measure. One must remember that gross monetary takings do not equate to broader public engagement, particularly when ticket price inflation and premium 3D surcharges skew the immediate comparisons.

What we are seeing across the early markets is simply extreme compression. When a release schedules screenings across twenty-four hours to satisfy months of pre-orders, it rapidly depletes the finite pool of highly engaged viewers. A film running one hundred and eighty-one minutes carries an inescapable logistical friction. Avatar achieved its historical position because its audience broadened progressively over dozens of consecutive weeks through casual, unhurried attendance. Processing millions of committed enthusiasts in a single four-day burst leaves the subsequent schedule heavily exposed. Once the local cinemas here settle into their standard operating hours next week, I expect the sudden drop in attendance will demonstrate how limited the general market truly is.

Today is the general UK release date, and seeing how the local venues manage the evening traffic will tell us a great deal.
