PintOfMild wrote:It's a quick sprint to hoover up the diehards, but once normal workweeks kick back in, that pace is completely unsustainable.
The overnight scheduling in the United States simply illustrates how severely front-loaded the release structure has become. Squeezing early screenings into the early morning hours exhausts the pool of enthusiastic viewers immediately. Once that initial cohort has attended, attendance must rely on general patrons who arrange their leisure around ordinary working weeks. Avatar accumulated its total across months of steady, unforced business. A three-day surge, regardless of how many temporary overnight slots are operated, cannot compensate for the absence of sustained weekly footfall.

by quasistatic » Tue Apr 23, 2019 9:48 am

There is also no need to see sinister corporate panic in these overnight rosters. It is standard operational efficiency: when extraordinary pre-sales demonstrate immediate local demand, exhibitors naturally add marginal capacity during dormant hours to maximize their receipts while the interest is acute.

However, that operational adjustment does not alter the underlying demographic arithmetic. Once the dedicated audience has attended their 3 a.m. screenings, subsequent weekends depend entirely on broader public habits. Without the multi-month, casual appeal that carried Avatar, that initial burst simply leaves fewer prospective attendees for May.
