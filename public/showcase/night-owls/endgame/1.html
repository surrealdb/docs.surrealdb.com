Quite so. It reminds me a little of the rush we had back in '79 when we migrated the clearing system. Management looked at the initial transaction spike on Monday morning and declared it an unqualified triumph, only to discover by Thursday that half the volume was simply the previous week's backlogged cheques clearing through at once. A very different sort of accounting, to be sure, but early enthusiasm often mistakes front-loaded demand for permanent momentum.

Two point seven billion dollars is a formidable mountain to climb. Sustaining that over May and June is another matter entirely, particularly with a running time exceeding three hours. That will naturally restrict the number of daily screenings a cinema can manage, regardless of how many auditoriums they give over to it.

by quasistatic » Fri Apr 19, 2019 6:03 am

The question of casual audiences is very relevant here. When Avatar had its run, it was an entirely self-contained novelty that drew people who rarely went to the cinema at all, and it kept drawing them well into the spring. A concluding chapter that leans on a decade of backstory is an entirely different proposition for anyone who has not kept up.

That said, I would be careful about writing off the sheer scale of the opening before we see the confirmed takings. Tracking in the United States is already pointing towards something in the region of 250 million dollars for the first three days, and the pre-sale figures from China suggest an enormous international turnout as well. Those are extraordinary numbers by any metric, even if a significant proportion is simply concentrated enthusiasm from the core audience.

My nephew has booked tickets for us in Edinburgh for the following week, so I shall be doing my best to avoid the leaks in the meantime. I am curious to see the actual Comscore returns once the weekend concludes, rather than the studio forecasts. Until then, enthusiasm is running well ahead of the evidence.
