by quasistatic » Fri Apr 26, 2019 8:24 am

TheyKnow wrote:Kev, you are being way too trusting if you think Disney running these turnstiles day and night without stopping is just ordinary business.
There is no corporate intrigue required to explain why exhibitors schedule overnight screenings when demand spikes; it is straightforward capacity management. If people are queuing to pay, venues naturally add showtimes.

That said, the international returns of $169 million on day one simply reflect the same front-loading we saw in the domestic presales. Meeting unprecedented demand across Korea, Australia, and China by utilizing round-the-clock schedules is an impressive operational feat, but it remains an extraordinary measure. Once that initial rush subsides and standard operational hours resume, the substantial runtime will inevitably assert itself as a deterrent for casual cinemagoers. We must wait for the post-weekend holds to see true retention.

It is not a silly observation at all. What you are describing is the standard mathematical pattern for highly anticipated franchise installments.

When a film relies almost entirely on advance bookings, exhibitors clear the vast majority of their customer base during the opening seventy-two hours. Once that committed audience has attended, the remaining pool consists of uncommitted viewers. For those individuals, allocating more than three hours of leisure time presents a clear disincentive. A steep decline from Monday onward is the logical outcome, and without steady secondary footfall, surpassing Avatar's total admissions remains quite impossible.
