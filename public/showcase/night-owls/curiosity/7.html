The landing team got a long standing ovation at JPL's news conference, with chants of 'EDL! EDL!'. John Holdren called it the most challenging mission ever attempted in robotic planetary exploration. NewsMon Aug 6, 2012 10:00 am GMT · Darmstadt, GermanyEurope's Mars Express recorded the landing signalsnot recalled by anyone

ESA says Mars Express tracked Curiosity's signals for about 28 minutes and lost them, as expected, just before touchdown. ESA's stations in New Norcia (Australia) and Cebreros (Spain) also picked up the signals directly, 248 million km away. The data went straight to JPL. ESA's Paolo Ferri congratulated NASA and said the Mars Express team welcomes 'a new friend in the neighbourhood'. Source
