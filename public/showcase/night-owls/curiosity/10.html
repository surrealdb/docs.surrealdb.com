by OffByOne » Thu Aug 9, 2012 6:43 am

Actually, the Mastcam assembly does not employ simple hinges or pins, but multi-axis motorized deployment actuators with dedicated resolvers to verify angular position.

Regarding the timing, downlink latency depends heavily on Mars Odyssey and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter overflights, which occur only a few times per Martian sol. News reports indicate the mast was indeed raised successfully during Sol 2, providing a 360-degree navigation mosaic. However, photographs alone remain insufficient. A sound technical judgment still requires the raw voltage, current, and temperature telemetry from those actuators to prove the mechanism operates strictly within design tolerances.

quasistatic wrote:What matters for the rover's long-term science return is how smoothly those actuators drew current during the elevation sequence, and whether the mechanical load remained within standard margins.
Strictly speaking, the descent stage engines did not blast the surface 'nearby' in a random fashion, but directly beneath their fixed cant angles during the final descent deceleration. The resulting scours merely reflect localized regolith displacement.

quasistatic outlines the exact engineering requirement. An upright mast and pleasant photographs show only the final geometric state, not the mechanical strain incurred during the sweep. Motor draw peaks, resolver transition counts, and thermal dissipation logs across the elevation sequence will determine whether the drive train suffered any binding or excess friction. Until those hex packets are parsed from the Deep Space Network feeds, patience is mandatory.
