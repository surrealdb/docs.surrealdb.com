OffByOne is quite correct. Framing systematic aerospace engineering as either an audacious gamble or a reckless wager merely clouds the issue. The merit of the expenditure rests on whether the rover can deliver verifiable geochemical analysis from Gale Crater's sedimentary record, nothing more.

To keep matters on track for tomorrow morning, there are three practical milestones to watch for:

1. Entry interface and the deceleration sequence down through the atmospheric friction.
2. Successful acquisition of the real-time carrier signal relayed via Mars Odyssey around 05:31 UTC.
3. The return of the initial engineering telemetry confirming the rover's autonomous health on the regolith.

Until those direct signals arrive, speculation serves little purpose.

Re: Curiosity lands on Mars on Monday morning - who's watching?

by TheyKnow » Sun Aug 5, 2012 1:21 pm

Breaking it down into clean bullet points does not make the danger any less real, folks... Writing off the risk as mere routine procedure sounds fine in a textbook, but out there you have a thousand things that can go sideways in an instant, and once that entry starts, Earth is completely out of the loop. If that direct view drops away and Odyssey fails to catch the carrier tone clean, you will not have any engineering telemetry to analyze at all. You are betting the farm on an autonomous sequence executing perfectly down to the millisecond without a human hand on the wheel. When that much tax money is on the line, calling it a gamble is not just dramatic sentiment, it is plain common sense. If that rig hits the ground wrong, those budget cuts folks keep mentioning will get an awful lot deeper, and there will not be any science program left to justify the expense. We will see soon enough whether all that complexity holds up under the strain, but nobody should pretend it is just an ordinary day at the office.
