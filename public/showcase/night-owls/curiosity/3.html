PintOfMild wrote:Spot on with that! You've got to take a proper punt if you want to achieve anything grand like this.
Taking a proper punt sounds fine down at the betting shop, my friend, but here we are talking about two and a half billion dollars of public money... That is an enormous amount of wealth to gamble on an untested stunt like a rocket crane lowering fragile machinery on cords.

When folks say you have to take big chances to achieve great things, well, that is fine poetry, but back here in the real world that cash could have done immense good right on the ground. Instead, the whole venture is riding on an intricate descent sequence where dozens of pyrotechnics and thrusters have to execute perfectly without human intervention, all while depending on narrow radio links through older orbiters just to hear if the thing is even in one piece. If that cable snags, or the dust and rocket wash fouls up an arm joint before it ever rolls an inch, all that investment simply vanishes into the rocks. It is not being overly pessimistic to point out that staking that much treasure on an all-or-nothing roll of the dice is a terrible waste of resources.

It really is out of everyone's hands now anyway, so treating it like a wasted gamble before it even touches down just feels miserable for no good reason. The money spent went into the pockets of thousands of brilliant scientists and engineers who designed something completely extraordinary, not fired off into the void to burn. And having the courage to fund real planetary science is what actually pushes human knowledge forward.
