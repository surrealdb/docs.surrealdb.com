Re: Curiosity lands on Mars on Monday morning - who's watching?

by OffByOne » Mon Aug 6, 2012 5:33 am

The announcement has just come through from JPL operations: touchdown is confirmed via the Canberra tracking station. The sky crane manoeuvre executed its programmed descent sequence, and the vehicle is resting on the surface inside Gale Crater.

It is certainly an impressive achievement in autonomous systems engineering. One must credit the automated guidance routines for executing such a complex mechanical sequence without human intervention. However, a successful landing is merely the physical precondition for the actual programme.

Now begins the systematic verification of the platform. We must see whether the hazard avoidance cameras transmit their initial telemetry through Odyssey, whether the power bus remains nominal, and how the internal components withstood the mechanical deceleration. Until the engineering staff can verify system health and deploy the main mast over the coming days, we have no substantive confirmation of the rover's scientific viability. A functional landing gear does not yet equal an operational analytical laboratory.

Re: Curiosity lands on Mars on Monday morning - who's watching?

by quasistatic » Mon Aug 6, 2012 5:37 am

The arrival of that first thumbnail from the rear hazard avoidance camera showing the wheel on the Martian surface is an immense relief. To see the sky crane deposit a metric ton of machinery into Gale Crater and clear the area safely is an outstanding engineering achievement by the JPL team, and everyone who designed that descent software deserves proper credit.

At the same time, we must maintain perspective as the celebration unfolds on the stream. A rover intact on the regolith merely provides the mobile chassis. The substantial scientific justification for this mission rests entirely upon whether the complex analytical payload survived that violent transition intact. The internal spectrometers, the sampling arm, and the geochemical laboratory inside the chassis are far more sensitive to shock and thermal stress than a ruggedised aluminium wheel.

We will not have true confirmation of the rover's scientific capability until the mast is raised, the main communication antennae are deployed, and the instruments run through their diagnostic routines over the coming sols. It is a splendid morning for aerospace engineering, but the scientific return remains entirely to be proven.

Small correction: the rover mass is not quite a metric ton. The flight weight of Curiosity is approximately 899 kilograms, or 900 kilograms if one rounds conventionally. That is certainly substantially heavier than the previous rovers, but precision in mass calculations matters when discussing kinetic energy transfer.

That minor detail aside, the subsequent operational phases will require an enormous amount of patience. Transmitting a single 64 by 64 pixel thumbnail through a UHF link on Mars Odyssey merely confirms the camera optics cleared the dust cover and the basic data bus functioned during relay. The actual analytical hardware - namely the internal ovens, the gas chromatograph, and the robotic turret drill - must undergo extensive cold-starts and baseline calibrations before any geological findings can be considered valid. In the Netherlands we have a sensible saying, 'men moet de huid niet verkopen voor de beer geschoten is'. Delivering the laboratory to the surface of Gale Crater is an undeniable triumph of aerospace logistics, but until the instrument spectra return verifiable data, the mission's ultimate return on investment remains completely undecided.
