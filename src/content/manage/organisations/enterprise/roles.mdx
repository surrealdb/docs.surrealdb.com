# Enterprise roles and permissions

A role is a named set of permissions. You compose the roles your enterprise needs, then assign them to [members](/docs/manage/organisations/enterprise/members).

An enterprise defines its own roles rather than choosing from a fixed set, so a role can grant exactly the administration one job needs.

## Enterprise roles and organisation roles

An enterprise holds two sets of roles at once, and they govern different things.

| | Organisation roles | Enterprise roles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What they are** | Owner, Admin, and Member, a fixed set | Roles you compose yourself |
| **What they govern** | Instances, billing, usage, and organisation membership | Enterprise members, roles, domains, single sign-on, and settings |
| **Where they are set** | The **Team** section of the organisation | The **Roles** page of the enterprise |
| **Documented in** | [Members and roles](/docs/manage/organisations/members-and-roles) | This page |

A person can hold both. An enterprise role that grants member administration says nothing about who may deploy an instance, and an organisation Admin holds no enterprise permissions until you grant them a role here.

## Create a role

1. Open **Roles** in your enterprise.
2. Start a new role.
3. Enter a **Name**, for example `Engineering`.
4. Enter a **Description**, which helps when two roles grant similar things.
5. Select the permissions the role grants.
6. Save the role.

A role grants nothing until it holds at least one permission, so an empty role cannot be saved.

Edit a role at any time. A change applies to every member holding that role.

## What a role can grant

The role editor lists the permissions your enterprise can grant, grouped by the area each one applies to. Read the editor for the current list, and use the table below to plan.

| Area | Permissions | What they cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Members** | Read, add, update, remove | Seeing the member list, inviting people, changing the roles a member holds, and ending a membership. |
| **Roles** | Read, create, update, delete | Seeing roles, and composing or removing them. |
| **Domains** | Read, create, verify, delete | Seeing domains, adding one, verifying ownership, and removing one. |
| **Single sign-on** | Read, create, delete, route domains, set applications | Seeing connections, adding or removing one, choosing which domains route to it, and choosing which applications it may be used with. |
| **Settings** | Update | Changing the display name, the identifiers, and the sign-in branding. |

Permission to read an area and permission to change it are granted separately. A role can therefore let someone audit single sign-on without letting them alter a connection.

## The owner

The person the enterprise belongs to is its **owner**, and holds every permission without being assigned a role. You cannot reduce an owner's access by changing their roles.

## Least privilege

Be sure to grant the smallest set of permissions needed to let a person do their work.

Three permissions deserve particular care, because each one changes who can reach your data:

- **Add members** admits a new person to the enterprise.
- **Create and delete single sign-on connections** decides which identity provider authenticates your company.
- **Verify domains** decides which email addresses are routed to a connection.

Give those to the people who administer identity at your company, and use a read-only role for everyone who needs to see the configuration without changing it.

Every page in the enterprise stays listed whatever role you hold. A page you cannot read names the permission it needs instead of its contents, so a member can tell you what to grant them.
