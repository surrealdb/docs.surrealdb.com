# Single sign-on

Single sign-on lets people at your company sign in to SurrealDB with the credentials they already hold, instead of creating a SurrealDB account of their own.

Sign-in is routed by email domain. A person enters their email address, SurrealDB matches the domain against the connections you have configured, and sends them to your identity provider to authenticate. Your identity provider decides whether they may sign in, and returns them to SurrealDB.

Three things must be in place before anyone is routed, and each has a step below:

1. A **domain** you have verified, which proves your company owns the email addresses.
2. A **connection** to your identity provider, which holds the credentials and endpoints.
3. A **route** from the domain to the connection, which ties the two together.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A connection on its own changes nothing. Until a verified domain routes to it, everyone continues to sign in the way they did before.

## Step 1: Verify an email domain

A verified domain is what lets single sign-on recognise your company's email addresses. Add the domain your company's addresses use, then prove you own it with a DNS record.

1. Open **Domains** in your enterprise.
2. Select **Add domain**.
3. Enter the domain, for example `example.com`.
4. Select **Add domain** to save it.

Studio then shows a DNS record for the domain. Add that record to your DNS, with the name and value exactly as shown, and then:

1. Wait for the record to propagate.
2. Select **Verify** beside the domain.

The domain is marked as verified when the record is found. If it is not found yet, Studio says so and you can try again. DNS changes can take a while to reach every resolver, so a verification that fails immediately after you add the record is worth repeating later.

> [!NOTE]
> The DNS record is shown only while the domain is unverified. Copy it before you verify, if you need to hand it to whoever administers your DNS.

Removing a domain stops any connection routing it from matching that domain, and people at that domain go back to signing in as they did before.

## Step 2: Add a connection

A connection holds the credentials and endpoints of your identity provider. Choose the type first, because each provider asks for different values and names them differently.

1. Open **Single sign-on** in your enterprise.
2. Select **Add connection**.
3. Choose your identity provider from the grid.
4. Enter a **Display name**. Members see this name on the sign-in screen.
5. Fill in the fields for that provider, then save.

### Supported providers

| Provider | What it is | Fields it asks for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Okta** | Okta Workforce | Okta domain, client ID, client secret |
| **Microsoft Entra ID** | Azure Active Directory | Directory domain, client ID, client secret |
| **Google Workspace** | Google Apps | Google Workspace domain, client ID, client secret |
| **ADFS** | Active Directory Federation Services | ADFS server URL |
| **PingFederate** | Ping Identity | Sign-in URL, sign-out URL, signing certificate |
| **Keycloak** | Keycloak SAML | Sign-in URL, sign-out URL, signing certificate |
| **SAML** | Generic SAML 2.0 | Sign-in URL, sign-out URL, signing certificate |
| **OpenID Connect** | Generic OIDC | Discovery URL, client ID, client secret |

Use **SAML** or **OpenID Connect** when your identity provider is not listed by name. Most identity providers speak one of the two, and a generic connection reaches the same result as a named one. The named entries exist to label the fields in the words your provider uses.

### What each field set needs

| Field | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| **Domain** | The directory domain your provider issues, such as `example.okta.com` or `example.onmicrosoft.com`. |
| **Client ID** and **Client secret** | The application you register with your identity provider for SurrealDB. |
| **Sign-in URL** | The endpoint your provider publishes for SAML sign-in requests. |
| **Sign-out URL** | The matching sign-out endpoint. It is optional. |
| **Signing certificate** | The PEM-encoded X.509 certificate your provider signs assertions with. |
| **Discovery URL** | The OpenID Connect discovery document, usually ending in `/.well-known/openid-configuration`. |

> [!WARNING]
> A client secret is a credential. Treat it the way you treat a password, and replace it through your identity provider if it is ever exposed.

## Step 3: Route domains to the connection

Routing decides who is sent to the connection.

1. Open the connection from the **Single sign-on** list.
2. Under **Sign-in domains**, select the verified domains to route.
3. Select **Save changes**.

People with an email address at those domains are then sent to this connection when they sign in. Only verified domains can be selected, so complete step 1 first.

You can route more than one domain to one connection, which suits a company that holds several email domains. Route each domain to the connection that authenticates it.

## Step 4: Choose the applications

A connection can be limited to some SurrealDB applications rather than all of them.

1. Open the connection.
2. Under **Applications**, turn each application on or off.
3. Select **Save changes**.

An application that is turned off refuses the connection, and people at your domains sign in to that application another way.

## How many connections you can add

The number of connections an enterprise may hold is set by its plan. When the enterprise has used all of them, **Add connection** is unavailable and says so.

If your plan includes no connections, the page explains that and offers a way to contact us. [Contact the SurrealDB team](https://surrealdb.com/contact) to change the plan.

## After single sign-on is working

People at a routed domain are sent to your identity provider whenever they sign in. Their membership then comes from your identity provider, and you grant their access inside SurrealDB with [roles](/docs/manage/organisations/enterprise/roles).

The [Members](/docs/manage/organisations/enterprise/members) page marks which members signed in through one of your own connections, so you can tell them apart from members holding an ordinary SurrealDB account.
